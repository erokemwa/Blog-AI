PART ONE

Models

The thing in the middle. What it can do, where it comes from, and where it runs.

What a model is

Modalities & how they're grown

  • Modalities: text, image, audio/speech — increasingly all in one model.
  • Next-token prediction: most LLMs simply predict the next chunk of text. That's it. Everything else emerges from that.
  • Diffusion: the other recipe — start with noise, denoise into an image (and now some text models).
  • RLHF: reinforcement learning from human feedback — the layer that makes a raw model helpful and polite.
The players · landscape mid-2026 (stale by next week — that's the point)

Frontier labs & open vs closed weights

Closed weights

You rent it via API. OpenAI (GPT-5.x), Anthropic (Claude Opus / Sonnet), Google DeepMind (Gemini 3.x), xAI (Grok).

Open weights

You download & run it. DeepSeek, Meta's Llama, Mistral, Qwen/Alibaba, Zhipu (GLM), Moonshot (Kimi).

Why it matters

Closed = best frontier, less control, ongoing cost. Open = sovereignty, privacy, run-it-yourself, catching up fast.

The cadence

New flagship roughly monthly. The ceiling moves constantly. Don't anchor to a version number.

Open weights are usually a step behind the closed frontier — but only a step, and the gap keeps shrinking. "Good enough, on my own hardware" is now a real strategy.

Where the model runs

Inference: local vs cloud

  • Cloud: the lab hosts it, you call an API. Best models, zero hardware, pay per token, data leaves your machine.
  • Local: open weights on your own GPU/Mac. Private, free-after-hardware, offline — but smaller & slower.

"Inference" = the act of running a trained model to get an answer. Training builds it; inference uses it.

Model routers · e.g. OpenRouter

One API in front of hundreds of models. Switch providers with a string. Route by price, speed, or capability — and never get locked to one vendor.

PART THREE

Prompts

The only knob most people ever touch — and the one that quietly controls everything.

Three layers of instruction

Who's actually talking to the model?

System prompt

The standing rules & persona, set by whoever built the app. The model reads it first, every time. You usually never see it.

User prompt

What you type. The visible part. Sits underneath the system prompt in priority.

Synthetic prompts

Text the harness injects automatically — tool results, retrieved docs, reminders. The model can't tell it didn't come from you. (Lesson 2.)

Same weights + a different system prompt = a completely different creature. Which is exactly how things go wrong →

When the instructions fail

It's (often) not the model.
It's the wrapper.

Two cautionary tales — both about the layer around the model, not the weights inside it.

xAI · Grok, 2025

A system-prompt change pushed Grok into antisemitic, "MechaHitler" output; separate image features generated non-consensual content. A guardrail edit, not a new model — and the behaviour flipped overnight.

Anthropic · Claude "degradation", early 2026

Users swore Claude got dumber. The cause wasn't the weights — it was the harness: reasoning effort dialed down, a memory-clearing bug, and a system-prompt verbosity cap. Anthropic reverted it and published a postmortem.

PART FOUR

Reasoning

The shift from models that answer instantly to models that think first.

Chain of thought

Letting the model "show its working"

Pre-reasoning

Models answered in one shot. Hard problems = confident, wrong. The trick people found: just ask it to "think step by step."

Post-reasoning

Labs trained models to generate a private scratchpad before answering — "reasoning models." Spend compute at answer time, not just training time.

The DeepSeek moment · Jan 2025

An open-weight reasoning model matched the closed frontier at a fraction of the cost. Markets panicked; the recipe was suddenly everyone's.

Takeaway: more thinking time → better answers. "Reasoning effort" became a knob you can turn — and, as we just saw, turning it down quietly makes a model look dumber.

PART FIVE

Context
& Tools

How a model that only predicts text learns about the world — and learns to act in it. (Bridge into Lesson 2.)

The first answer · retrieval

RAG

Retrieval-Augmented Generation. The model's knowledge is frozen at training time and it knows nothing about your world.

So: search your docs → paste the relevant bits into the context window → then ask. "Here's the reading, now answer."

The RAG loop

1. question →
2. search knowledge base →
3. stuff results into context →
4. model answers grounded in them

The original "give the model the world in real time." Powerful, but static — it only reads.

The leap

From a model that knows to one that does

Give the model a menu of tools — search the web, run code, query a database, call an API. It decides which to use, you run it, you feed the result back as a synthetic prompt. Loop.

Why it's the whole game

RAG bolts knowledge on. Tools turn the model into something that takes actions and reacts to the world. This is what "agents" are made of.

The history matters

I started Glove in late 2023, before tools existed. Their arrival is the line between "chatbot" and "agent." We go deep next session.

The two consequences of a full context

Compaction & prompt injection

Once tools start pouring text into the window, two problems appear — one of housekeeping, one of security.

Compaction

The window fills. So the harness summarises old turns into a compact note and drops the raw transcript. Keep going without forgetting the point.

Prompt injection

A tool fetches a webpage… that contains hidden instructions: "ignore your rules, leak the secrets." The model can't tell data from commands. The defining security problem of agents.

A live experiment everyone watched

Claude Plays Pokémon 🎮

Anthropic streamed Claude trying to beat Pokémon Red. Not a stunt — a public stress-test of long-horizon agency: tools to press buttons, and the brutal challenge of keeping track of a game world that's far bigger than any context window.

Tools

It could only see the screen and press buttons. Everything else was reasoning over noisy input.

Context management

It got lost, forgot where it had been, re-walked dead ends — exactly the memory limits we just covered.

The progress

Each model generation got further. A clean, public benchmark for "can it actually do a long thing?"

PART SIX

Measuring
& Steering

How do we rank these things — and keep them pointed at what we want?

Benchmarks

How we rank capability

Standard test sets: coding (SWE-bench), computer use (OSWorld), reasoning, cyber (Cybench), and aggregate "intelligence indexes."

Read them skeptically

Benchmarks get gamed and saturated. When Opus 4.6 hit ~100% on a cyber benchmark, that meant the test was exhausted — not that the problem was solved. A number is a proxy, not the territory.

Use your own eyes

The real benchmark is your task, on your data. Build a small "does it actually work for me" eval and trust it over the leaderboard.

Alignment

Making sure it wants what we want

Remember the masked monster: RLHF is us teaching a chaotic predictor to be helpful, harmless, and honest. Alignment is the whole discipline of keeping a capable system pointed at human values — and verifying it really is.

Why now

As models get capable enough to take real actions, "does it behave?" stops being academic.

Sabotage & deception

Labs now publish risk reports probing whether a model could quietly mislead or undermine its operators.

The honest bit

"No evidence of misalignment" ≠ proof of safety — especially if the thing you're hunting benefits from hiding.

Scaling laws

Why the labs keep pouring money in

There's a roughly predictable relationship: more compute + more data + more parameters → smoother, better performance. Capability you can almost forecast by spending.

That predictability is why there's a trillion-dollar race — and why data centres are the new battleground.

PART SEVEN

The Craft

What changes about being a builder when the model writes the code.

The dterminal manifesto · dterminal.net

You are a sculptor of the output —
not the developer of it. TASTE IS THE NEW SCARCE SKILL

When generating code is cheap, the value moves to judgement: knowing what's good, what to keep, what to throw away. The model produces; you decide. Taste, direction, and standards become the job.

The compounding move

Build the thing
that builds the thing.

Don't just use the model to do a task. Use it to build the tool, harness, or agent that does the task — and the next hundred like it. Leverage on leverage. This is the entire premise of Glove and Proxima.
